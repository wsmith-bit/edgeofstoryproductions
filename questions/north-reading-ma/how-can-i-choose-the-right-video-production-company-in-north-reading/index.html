1. Check Credentials and Experience

Start by looking for a video production company with a solid track record and relevant credentials. Check if they have industry awards or recognitions, such as an Emmy nomination, which Edge of Story Films received in 2024. This can indicate a commitment to quality and excellence in their work.

2. Review Their Portfolio

Ask to see samples of their previous work. A strong portfolio should showcase a variety of projects, including:

  • Corporate videos and brand films
  • Animated explainer videos
  • Testimonial videos from clients
  • Event coverage that highlights their ability to capture live moments

For businesses in North Reading, it’s particularly important that the company understands local industries, whether it's the growing number of tech startups or established financial services firms in the area.

3. Ask About Their Production Process

During your initial conversations, inquire about their end-to-end production capabilities. A comprehensive process should include:

  • Creative concept development
  • Filming in high-definition formats like 4K or 6K
  • Editing and post-production services, including motion graphics and animations
  • Final media delivery tailored to your needs

Understanding how they handle each stage of production can give you confidence that they can deliver a polished final product.

4. Evaluate Communication and Collaboration

Effective communication is key to a successful partnership. Look for a company that actively listens to your needs and is responsive to your inquiries. It helps to collaborate with someone who can guide you through the process, especially if you have internal team members who may be camera-shy or unsure about what they want.

5. Identify Red Flags

Be cautious of companies that:

  • Have a lack of transparency regarding pricing or timelines
  • Provide limited examples of their work or have poor reviews
  • Push for a quick decision without giving you time to evaluate options
  • Do not seem to understand your industry or local context
